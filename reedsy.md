I was introduced to Reedsy a couple of years ago by a friend. Having worked very successfully in-house at one of the world's largest publishers, she'd recently gone freelance and was enjoying the freedom. I'd never heard of the platform before.

'Reedsy?' she said. 'Oh yes, you can earn a living on there.'

I'd been freelance for some time by then, and while I appreciated the flexibility, I'd grown disillusioned with the enervating routine of invoicing clients for finished work, then chasing them... and chasing them, and chasing them.

Whole months could pass between completing a job and being paid for it. Not only was this frustrating, it made the necessary task of maintaining sufficient cashflow to pay bills and live extremely difficult. (At times, impossible.)

With Reedsy, it's different. The client and the professional agree the terms of a job -- including payment dates and deadline -- in advance. The client is asked to provide a payment method and the professional gets to work. When the payment dates come around, funds are transferred automatically. Simple as that.

To say that, for a freelance, this is a breath of fresh air would be an understatement. But there are real advantages for clients too. Since all communications with professionals happen on Reedsy's platform, there's a clear electronic 'paper trail'. If ever a client isn't satisfied that a job has been undertaken as agreed, Reedsy is in a position to mediate.

I wouldn't call it a perfect system; I doubt any such thing exists. But it's a thoroughly good idea, well implemented, and it works. Nowadays, almost all the work I do comes to me via Reedsy.
